Journey Overview

The Route to Promajna

Promajna sits a little over an hour from the airport, the drive covering the full stretch of coast road south through Omiš and past the smaller villages before opening onto Promajna's longer beach frontage. At 63 kilometres, it's solidly in the middle of the Makarska Riviera runs — far enough to feel like a proper journey, not so far that it needs a break along the way. The road widens noticeably as it approaches Promajna, reflecting the resort's larger scale compared with the smaller villages passed earlier in the drive, and the final stretch runs almost directly along the beachfront promenade before turning inland to most hotels and apartments. Traffic can build noticeably on Saturday changeover days in peak season, when accommodation across the whole riviera turns over at once, though this affects the final approach far more than the open road earlier in the drive. Outside those peak changeover windows, this remains one of the more relaxed drives on the whole itinerary, with the road opening into wide sea views for much of the second half of the journey.

About Promajna

What to Expect on the Ground

Promajna is one of the more developed resorts on this list, its beachfront lined with a mix of family-run guesthouses and mid-sized hotels behind a long run of sandy-pebble beach that's genuinely well-suited to families. The promenade connects Promajna to the neighbouring settlement of Bratuš with barely a gap, giving visitors a longer stretch of café-lined waterfront to walk than the smaller villages further along this coast can offer. It's busier in July and August than Pisak or Marušići, but still calmer than Makarska or Tučepi, sitting in a comfortable middle ground between quiet and convenient. A reasonable spread of supermarkets, bakeries and rental agencies along the main road makes Promajna one of the more self-sufficient bases on the riviera, useful for longer stays where a daily trip into Makarska isn't always practical. The resort grew steadily through the 1980s and 1990s as domestic Yugoslav tourism gave way to a broader international market, and much of today's accommodation dates from that period — solid, unfussy buildings rather than architectural showpieces, which suits a destination built around beach time rather than sightseeing. Water sports operators along the beachfront offer everything from jet-ski hire to banana-boat rides through the main season, giving Promajna a livelier daytime beach atmosphere than some of the quieter villages further along this same stretch of coast, particularly popular with families travelling with older children and teenagers.

What to Expect During the Transfer

Real-time flight tracking means the driver adjusts pickup automatically if your flight lands late, even by several hours. From the terminal it's a direct run down the coast road, luggage loaded once and unloaded at your accommodation, with no shared shuttle stops along the way. Promajna's larger size compared with the smaller villages nearby means street numbering is more straightforward, but with several hotels and apartment blocks sharing similar names along the promenade, it's still worth confirming your exact property when booking to avoid any confusion on arrival. Groups arriving together for a family holiday will find the Business Van a practical option, with enough room for luggage, beach gear and car seats where needed, all for one fixed price regardless of how many passengers are travelling on the day.

Popular Nearby

Places Worth Knowing About

Baška Voda is a short drive north, and Tučepi and Marušići are both within easy reach to the south, making Promajna a workable base for exploring a fair stretch of the riviera without constantly changing accommodation. Makarska itself, with its ferry connections and larger selection of restaurants, is close enough for a straightforward day or evening trip. The promenade linking Promajna to Bratuš and on toward Baška Voda makes for a pleasant early evening walk with several ice cream stops along the way, and a handful of small supermarkets and bakeries dotted along the main road cover most day-to-day needs without a special trip into Makarska. For a change of pace, the ferry from Makarska to Sumartin on the island of Brač runs regularly through the season, making a full island day trip a realistic option for anyone staying in Promajna for more than a few days, and the crossing itself offers some of the best views back toward Biokovo from open water. Brač's own beaches, including the well-known Zlatni Rat near Bol, make a worthwhile full-day excursion for visitors keen to see more of the wider region during a longer holiday.

Common Questions

What is the fixed price for a transfer to Promajna?

€115 one-way in the Sedan (1–4 passengers) or €165 in the Business Van or Van (1–7–8 passengers), fixed for the full 63 km journey.

Is Promajna a good base for families?

Yes — the long sandy-pebble beach and gentle promenade make it one of the more family-friendly stops on the Makarska Riviera.

How busy does Promajna get in peak season?

It's busier than the smaller villages nearby in July and August, but noticeably quieter than Makarska or Tučepi town centres.

Can I combine a Promajna transfer with a stop in Baška Voda?

Let us know when booking if you'd like a short stop en route — the driver can factor in extra time where the route allows.

Do you offer group vehicles for larger families visiting Promajna?

Yes — the Business Van and Van both take up to seven or eight passengers with luggage, priced as a single fixed fare regardless of how full the vehicle is.
