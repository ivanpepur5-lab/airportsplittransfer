Journey Overview

The Route to Seget Donji

Seget Donji is about as close to the airport as any destination on this coast — eight kilometres and around twelve minutes, most of it spent on the short stretch of road that runs along the water toward Trogir before turning off just short of the old town bridge. It's less a journey than a quick hop, but the fixed price and flight-tracking still apply exactly as they would for a longer run. The road passes directly along Kaštela Bay for most of its short length, giving arriving guests one of the fastest and most scenic routes from the terminal anywhere on this list, with Čiovo island and Trogir's bell tower visible ahead for the final couple of minutes. For visitors on a tight connection — an afternoon flight followed by an evening ferry, for instance — this short transfer time is often the deciding factor in choosing Seget Donji over a base further along the coast.

About Seget Donji

What to Expect on the Ground

Seget Donji is effectively Trogir's western neighbour, a strip of hotels, apartments and campsites that has grown up along the coast road within easy walking distance — or a very short drive — of the UNESCO-listed old town. It doesn't have much of an old centre of its own, and that's rarely the point of staying here: the appeal is proximity, a base that puts Trogir's stone streets, the Kamerlengo fortress and the town's waterfront restaurants a few minutes away, without paying old-town prices for accommodation. The coastline here is a mix of small pebble beaches and hotel-managed swimming areas, functional rather than dramatic, built for convenience. Several larger resort complexes along this stretch run their own pools and beach clubs, making Seget Donji a practical choice for travellers who want Trogir's sights by day and a quieter, more spread-out base to return to in the evening. The area has expanded considerably since the 1970s as package tourism grew around Trogir, and today's mix of older campsites and newer apartment developments reflects several decades of gradual, largely unplanned growth along this stretch of coast road — a contrast with Trogir's own tightly protected historic core just a few minutes away, where building has been essentially frozen for centuries. This mix of old and new gives Seget Donji a genuinely practical character, popular with repeat visitors who know exactly what they want from a Trogir-area stay.

What to Expect During the Transfer

Given the short distance, your driver will already be close by once your flight lands — we still track your flight number, so even a fast turnaround accounts for any delay. It's a direct, private run with your luggage handled once at each end, no shared shuttle and no detour through other pickups. Because so many Seget Donji properties are large resort complexes rather than individually numbered houses, it's worth naming the specific hotel or apartment complex clearly when booking — reception buildings in this area can sit a reasonable walk from the main road, and a driver who knows the exact entrance saves genuine time compared with a generic drop-off at the roadside. This short route is also a common choice for guests connecting onward to a ferry from Split, where a fixed, reliable pickup time matters more than the length of the drive itself, and the driver can build in extra margin on request if your onward connection is tight.

Popular Nearby

Places Worth Knowing About

Trogir's old town is immediately next door, walkable from most parts of Seget Donji. Vinišće's quieter bay is a short drive up the coast, and Marina Frapa in Rogoznica is within easy reach for a day out if you're staying in the area for more than a couple of nights. The island of Čiovo, connected to Trogir by a small bridge, has its own quieter beaches and a scattering of restaurants worth the short detour, particularly in the evening when Trogir's old town itself gets busy with day-trippers. For anyone staying a full week or more, Split itself remains within easy reach for a change of scenery, with its own old town and waterfront markets a straightforward onward transfer away. Kaštela, the string of small towns lining the bay between Split and Trogir, is also worth a mention for anyone interested in the area's history — each Kaštel takes its name from the fortified manor houses built to defend against Ottoman raids, several of which still stand today, open to visitors along the bay.
