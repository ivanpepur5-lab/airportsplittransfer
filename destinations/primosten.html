Journey Overview

The Route to Primošten

Split Airport sits at the southern end of the same stretch of coast Primošten sits on, so the drive is a single run north along the motorway and coastal road through Kaštela Bay, past Trogir and Rogoznica, before turning off onto the narrow spit of land that leads out to Primošten's peninsula. It's a longer trip than the airport's closest destinations, but still a modest one by the standards of this coastline — typically somewhere around forty-five minutes to an hour outside the busiest summer traffic, depending on which of the two road options your driver takes on the day. Because the route runs the same corridor as several other well-known stops on this coast, it's an easy one to combine with a stop elsewhere, and just as straightforward in reverse for a flight departure, provided the usual allowance for summer weekend traffic through Kaštela Bay.

About Primošten

What to Expect on the Ground

Primošten began as a small fortified island just off the mainland, and stayed that way for centuries until a narrow causeway was built across the strait in the sixteenth century, originally to help the town defend itself against Ottoman raids further inland. That causeway is still how every visitor arrives today, leading straight onto a peninsula so tightly packed with red-roofed stone houses that the streets narrow to little more than stairways as they climb toward the top. The parish Church of St. George crowns the highest point, its terrace giving an open view back across the water to the mainland and out over the bay in the other direction — reason enough on its own for the climb. Around the town, the hillsides are covered in a landscape found almost nowhere else on the coast: dry-stone-walled vineyard terraces known locally as "ograde", built up over generations from cleared rock, where the indigenous Babić grape has been grown for red wine since long before Primošten became a destination in its own right. Just south of the old town, Mala Raduča is one of the better-known pebble beaches on this stretch of coast, backed by pine woods and a run of holiday resorts that make the wider Primošten area a popular family base in summer.

What to Expect During the Transfer

Your driver tracks your flight number from touchdown, so the transfer starts with someone already waiting at arrivals rather than a search for transport once you're through customs. Because Primošten sits a little further from the airport than some of the coast's closer stops, it's worth confirming your exact drop-off — a hotel or resort address, a specific street in the old town, or a berth in the harbour — when you book, since the causeway itself is closed to general traffic and the old town's own streets are on foot only. If you're staying at one of the resorts or campsites along the coast either side of the peninsula rather than in the old town itself, give your driver the property name directly; the area covers a longer stretch of coastline than the old town alone, and an exact address avoids any confusion on arrival. For a return flight, build in slightly more margin than you would from one of the airport's very closest destinations, particularly on a summer Saturday, when resort changeover traffic adds to the ordinary load on the coastal road.

Getting Around

Once You're in Primošten

The old town itself is entirely car-free once you're past the causeway, so a private transfer drops you at the small square and car park just before it, leaving a short walk in to wherever you're staying or eating. From there, the climb to the Church of St. George at the top takes most visitors ten to fifteen unhurried minutes, and the view is worth timing for early evening once the day's heat has passed. Beyond the old town, the vineyard hills inland are crossed by narrow lanes that make for an easy walk or drive if you want to see the stone-terraced "ograde" up close, and several small family wineries around the town sell Babić directly from the cellar. Along the coast either side of the peninsula, Mala Raduča and the smaller coves further out are the easiest beaches to reach without a car, while July and August bring the town's busiest stretch, when the old town's narrow lanes and harbour restaurants fill with both resort guests and day visitors from further along the coast.

Common Questions

How far is Primošten from Split Airport, and how long does the drive take?

Primošten sits roughly 35 kilometres north of the airport along the coastal road and motorway — the exact distance depends on the route — with the drive typically taking somewhere around forty-five minutes to an hour outside peak season traffic.

How much does a transfer from Split Airport to Primošten cost?

The exact price depends on your pickup and drop-off address. Enter both in our booking form to see an instant, fixed price for the Sedan or for the Business Van/Van — tolls, fuel and meet-and-greet at arrivals are always included.

Can the driver take me straight to a hotel or campsite outside the old town?

Yes — most of Primošten's accommodation is spread along the coast on either side of the peninsula rather than inside the car-free old town itself, so give us the exact property name when you book and your driver will take you directly there.

Is the old town walkable, or is it far from where I'd be dropped off?

Primošten's old town sits on a peninsula reached by a single causeway closed to general traffic, so a transfer drops you at the car park just before it — from there, the church at the top and everything in between is a short, if occasionally steep, walk.

Can I combine a transfer to Primošten with a stop in Rogoznica or Trogir?

Yes, Primošten sits on the same coastal route as Rogoznica and Trogir, so a multi-stop request is easy to arrange. Mention any additional stop when you book and we'll confirm it as part of the same fixed-price journey.
