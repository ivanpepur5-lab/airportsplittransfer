Journey Overview

The Route to Trogir

Split Airport sits on the mainland almost directly across the channel from Trogir, which makes this one of the shortest transfers we run anywhere on the coast. The drive follows the coastal road south through Kaštela Bay before crossing onto the small island that holds Trogir's old town, connected to the mainland and to Čiovo island by a pair of short bridges either side. Outside the busiest summer weekends, the whole journey typically takes only ten to fifteen minutes — short enough that some arriving guests are genuinely surprised how quickly the transfer is over. It also works well in reverse for a flight departure: because the drive back to the terminal is so short, there's very little buffer needed even for an early morning check-in, which matters more than it sounds once you've experienced a 4am airport run on a longer route elsewhere on this coast.

About Trogir

What to Expect on the Ground

Trogir's entire old town occupies a small island barely a few hundred metres across, wedged between the Croatian mainland and Čiovo, and it has been continuously inhabited since Greek settlers first built here more than two thousand years ago. UNESCO listed the whole historic centre as a World Heritage Site in 1997, citing it as one of the best-preserved Romanesque-Gothic complexes in Central Europe. The Cathedral of St. Lawrence dominates the main square, its west portal — carved by the medieval sculptor Radovan — considered one of the finest surviving works of its kind on the Adriatic. A short walk away, the fifteenth-century Kamerlengo Fortress guards the waterfront where the old town meets the sea, its walls open to visitors and worth the climb for the view back across the harbour. Between the two, a dense grid of stone streets barely wide enough for two people to pass holds cafés, small galleries and family-run konobas that stay busy well into the evening. The riva along the Čiovo channel is where most of Trogir's daily life plays out — a working promenade lined with yachts, charter boats and the occasional superyacht, since Trogir has become one of the more significant charter bases on this part of the coast alongside Split and Rogoznica.

What to Expect During the Transfer

Your driver tracks your flight number from the moment you land, so even a short hop like this one starts with someone waiting at arrivals rather than a scramble to find transport. Because the drive is so brief, this route works particularly well for guests who'd rather spend their travel budget on the actual holiday than on a long transfer — and just as well for a same-day arrival straight onto a charter yacht, since the short drive leaves plenty of the afternoon free for provisioning or a first walk around the old town before boarding. If you're being dropped at a marina rather than an address in the old town, give the exact berth, pontoon or charter company name when you book; drivers who run this route regularly know the Trogir marinas well and will take you straight to the right gangway rather than the general car park. For return journeys to the airport, the short drive means there's rarely any need to build in a large safety margin, though summer weekend changeover traffic through Kaštela Bay can still add a few minutes worth planning around for an early flight.

Getting Around

Once You're in Trogir

Trogir's old town is fully pedestrian, so a private transfer will drop you at the edge of it — typically near the bridge from the mainland or the bus station car park — rather than driving into the historic core itself, which cars can't enter regardless. From there, everything worth seeing is within a five-minute walk: the cathedral, the fortress and the riva form a compact triangle that most visitors cover comfortably in an afternoon. Across the southern bridge, Čiovo island offers a quieter, more residential alternative with its own small beaches and a handful of hotels away from the old town's crowds, and is where several of the larger marina operations and boatyards are actually based. Trogir gets genuinely busy with day-trip tour groups from Split between late morning and mid-afternoon in July and August; arriving earlier in the day, or staying overnight in Trogir itself, is the easiest way to see the old town without the crowds.

Popular Nearby

Places Worth Knowing About

Split's old town and Diocletian's Palace are only a short drive in the other direction, easily combined with a Trogir stop on the same private transfer if your schedule allows. Along the Trogir Riviera towards the airport and beyond, the quieter villages of Vinišće and Seget Donji make an easy extension for travellers staying slightly outside Trogir itself, both a short drive further along the same coast road. Marina Frapa in Rogoznica is a similar charter base a little further north, worth knowing about if you're comparing marinas for a sailing trip. And for a longer day out inland, Plitvice Lakes National Park or the closer waterfalls at Krka National Park are both reachable as a full-day private excursion from a Trogir base.

How far is Trogir from Split Airport?

Trogir is one of the closest destinations to the airport on the whole coast — only a few kilometres by road, with the drive typically taking around ten to fifteen minutes outside peak traffic.

How much does a transfer from Split Airport to Trogir cost?

The exact price depends on your pickup and drop-off address. Enter both in our booking form to see an instant, fixed price for the Sedan or for the Business Van/Van — tolls, fuel and meet-and-greet at arrivals are always included.

Can the driver drop me at a marina or charter base in Trogir?

Yes — Trogir is a major charter and superyacht base, and drivers regularly drop sailing guests directly at the marina reception or a specific pontoon. Give the exact berth or company name in the booking notes.

Is Trogir walkable, or is the old town far from where I'd be dropped off?

Trogir's old town sits on a small island only a few hundred metres across, so once you're dropped at the edge of it everything — the cathedral, the fortress, the riva — is within a short walk.

Can I combine a Trogir transfer with a stop in Split or Seget Donji?

Yes, multi-stop requests are common on this route since Split, Trogir and Seget Donji sit so close together. Mention any additional stop when you book and we'll confirm it as part of the same fixed-price journey.
