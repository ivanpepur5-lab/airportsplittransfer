Journey Overview

The Route to Pisak

The drive to Pisak follows the coast road south past Omiš, where the route narrows and starts to climb along the base of the cliffs that define this whole stretch of the Makarska Riviera. It's one of the more scenic legs on this list — the road threads between rock face and sea for long sections, with the water rarely out of view. Small pull-offs along the way mark some of the best viewpoints on the whole riviera, where the road briefly widens above a sheer drop to the water — not a place to stop in a rental car unfamiliar with the road, but a genuine highlight when someone else is driving. At 52 kilometres and around 50 minutes, it's long enough to properly settle in and enjoy the drive rather than just endure it. This stretch of coast road was cut into the cliffside decades ago and remains one of the more dramatic pieces of civil engineering on the Croatian Adriatic, with sections held up by retaining walls directly above the water — worth appreciating from the passenger seat rather than from behind the wheel, particularly in the late afternoon when the light along this section of coast is at its best.

About Pisak

What to Expect on the Ground

Pisak is small even by the standards of this coastline — a cluster of houses and a handful of restaurants strung along the coast road, with a narrow pebble beach dropping away below. What it lacks in size it makes up for in setting: the cliffs behind press close to the water, and the beach itself catches long afternoon sun with the Biokovo massif rising directly behind. There's little in the way of organised tourism infrastructure here, which is exactly the point for visitors who've chosen it over the busier resorts further south — Pisak stays quiet even in August, a stop for people who know the road rather than a destination that advertises itself. The narrow strip of flat land between road and sea means most accommodation here is small-scale and family-run, often the same family renting out rooms above their own restaurant, which gives the village a genuine, unpolished character that's increasingly rare on this coast. The road above Pisak is regarded by many regular visitors to the Makarska Riviera as one of the most photogenic short stretches of the entire coastal route, with the cliffs, sea and mountain all visible at once from a handful of well-known pull-offs — a reason some travellers choose a private transfer over self-driving specifically so they can watch the view rather than the road.

What to Expect During the Transfer

Flight tracking means your pickup time adjusts automatically if your arrival is delayed, so there's no need to guess or call ahead. The drive down is direct and private, with a stop possible en route if you'd like to see the Omiš gorge — mention this when booking and the driver can factor in the extra time. Because Pisak is so small, exact addresses can be vague; giving the name of your accommodation or the nearest named restaurant along the coast road helps the driver find the right stretch quickly rather than doubling back along a road with limited passing space. Given the scenic nature of this drive, some guests choose to arrive a little earlier in the day specifically to enjoy the coast road in good light — a preference worth mentioning at booking if it applies to you.

Popular Nearby

Places Worth Knowing About

Marušići and Promajna are both a short further drive south along the same coast road, easy to combine into a single longer transfer or visit separately during a longer stay. Omiš itself, with its riverside old town and zipline attractions, sits just north and makes a natural day trip from a Pisak base. The Cetina river gorge above Omiš offers rafting and canyoning trips popular with visitors staying anywhere on this stretch of the riviera, generally booked a day or two in advance through operators based in Omiš itself. For a quieter alternative to organised activities, several small unmarked coves between Pisak and Omiš are reachable by a short scramble down from the coast road, known mostly to regular visitors rather than first-time guests. The fortress of Fortica above Omiš is also worth the climb for anyone interested in the region's history of piracy and defence against Ottoman incursions, with views back down over the river mouth that make the short hike worthwhile, best attempted in the cooler morning hours during summer.

FAQ

Common Questions

How much does a transfer from Split Airport to Pisak cost?

€100 one-way in the Sedan (1–4 passengers) or €145 in the Business Van or Van (1–7–8 passengers), fixed per vehicle for the full 52 km drive.

Can we stop in Omiš on the way down?

Yes, if arranged in advance — mention it in your booking notes and the driver will factor a short stop into the journey time.

Is the coast road to Pisak safe to drive in winter?

Yes, the road is maintained year-round, though winter storms occasionally bring short closures for high wind — your driver will always take the safest available route.

Is there anywhere to eat in Pisak itself?

Yes, a small number of konoba-style restaurants along the coast road serve the village and passing traffic, though options are limited compared with Omiš or Makarska.

How does Pisak compare to staying in Omiš or Makarska?

Pisak is quieter and smaller than both, better suited to travellers who want easy access to the coast road without resort-town crowds.
