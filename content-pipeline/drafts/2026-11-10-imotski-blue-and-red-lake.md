## Body

Behind the Biokovo mountain, away from the sea, lies the small town of Imotski. Most visitors know it for two things: the Blue Lake and the Red Lake,
two huge sinkholes just outside town. They look nothing like the Adriatic coast, and that is exactly why guests ask about them. This guide
explains what the lakes are, when they are worth a visit and how a private driver fits into the day.

### What the two lakes are

Both lakes sit in karst sinkholes, deep holes where the limestone has collapsed. The Blue Lake (Modro jezero) is a wide round basin with steep rock walls.
A travel news report gives the sinkhole a depth of about 220 metres from the rim, and says the water reaches around 90 metres in spring when the snow melts.
The Red Lake (Crveno jezero) is a narrower and deeper pit with sheer cliffs, named for the reddish colour of the rock. Both are fed from underground,
which is why their level changes so much over the year.

### The Blue Lake is not always full

This is the most useful thing to know before you go. The water level follows the seasons. In spring the lake is high and bright blue. In dry years
it can disappear almost completely in late summer. In 2020 the water was gone by the start of September, and the locals have a tradition of playing a football match on the dry lake bed
when that happens. So if swimming is the point of your trip, the safest months are late spring and early summer, and it is worth checking recent photos or asking at the
local tourist office for the current water level before you set out.

The Red Lake is different. It is seen from the viewpoints on the rim. There are no safe paths down to the water, and the slopes are steep, so do not plan to swim there.

### More than the lakes

Imotski itself is worth an hour. The old town has stone streets, a small museum and the Topana fortress, a ruin on the hill above the centre with a view
over the town and the karst landscape. Many visitors walk from one lake to the other and finish with a coffee in town. The area is also known for its farms and local food,
so lunch is easy to arrange.

### The drive from Split

Imotski is inland, so the route leaves the coast and climbs. Most of the way is motorway, and the last stretch is a regional road through hills.
Count on roughly an hour from Split and a little more from the airport, depending on traffic and the road works that often appear in summer. Bus services exist but are slow,
around an hour and a half to two hours from Split, so a private car saves a large part of the day.

### Why go with a private driver

- **No parking search.** The driver drops you near the lakes and waits, so you walk the rim at your own pace.
- **Flexible timing.** Start early to avoid the summer heat and the busiest hours, and decide on the spot how long to stay at each viewpoint.
- **Luggage and groups.** If you travel on afterwards, your bags stay in the vehicle. A Mercedes V-Class takes up to 7 passengers and a Renault Trafic up to 8.
- **Fixed price.** We agree the price with you before the day, with fuel, tolls and parking included.

### Planning the day

Start in the morning. The viewpoints have little shade, and the light on the rock walls is best before midday. A simple plan is the drive out, the Blue Lake first,
then the Red Lake and the old town for lunch, and the drive back in the afternoon. Because the car waits, you can stretch or shorten each stop without watching a timetable.
If you are staying on the Makarska coast, Imotski lies on the other side of the Biokovo range, so tell us your hotel when you book and we plan the pickup and the return from there.

### Combining Imotski with another stop

Many guests ask for one more stop on the same day. Imotski is far from the coast crowds, so a lunch in town and a second sight fit easily. Tell us what you would like
to add and we plan the day around it. Our day trip pages cover Krka National Park, Plitvice Lakes and the Biokovo Skywalk, and we arrange custom private tours
to other places on request.

### What to bring

- Sturdy shoes. The paths along the rim are uneven.
- Water and a hat in summer, because there is little shade at the viewpoints.
- Swimwear and a towel if the Blue Lake is full.
- A light jacket in spring and autumn, when it is cooler inland than on the coast.

## FAQ (5)

**How far is Imotski from Split?**
Roughly an hour by car, a little more from Split Airport, depending on traffic and road works. Buses take around an hour and a half to two hours.

**Is the Blue Lake in Imotski always full?**
No. It fills in spring and can dry out in late summer in dry years. Check the current water level before you go if swimming is part of your plan.

**Can you swim in the Red Lake?**
No. The Red Lake is seen from the viewpoints on the rim. The cliffs are steep and there is no safe path down to the water.

**Do I have to pay to see the lakes?**
Recent travel sources describe the lakes as free to visit, but older pages mention a small ticket that also covered the fortress. Check locally before you go.

**Can you arrange a full day trip to Imotski?**
Yes. We arrange fully private custom tours. Send us your date, group size and the stops you want, and we agree a fixed price before the day.
