Split to Mostar: A Day Across the Border by Private Car

## Body

Mostar is the one place outside Croatia that many guests ask about before they have even left the airport. The reason is simple. The old town and its stone bridge are worth the trip, and from Split the journey is shorter than most people expect. Done with a private driver, it is an easy day with no timetable to chase.

### Why guests choose Mostar from Split

Mostar lies on the Neretva river in the Herzegovina region of Bosnia and Herzegovina. Its old bridge, Stari Most, was rebuilt and reopened in 2004 and the old town around it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The streets are lined with stone houses, small workshops and cafes, and the whole centre is built for walking.

Guests tend to fall into two groups. Some are staying in Split or on the Makarska coast and want one day somewhere different. Others are travelling on to Dubrovnik or further and would like to see Mostar on the way. Both work well by car, because the route is direct and a private driver can wait while you explore.

### The route and the border

The drive leaves the Dalmatian coast and climbs inland. The scenery changes from sea and olive groves to dry hills and river valleys. Expect roughly two and a half hours each way, depending on traffic and on the queue at the border. The distance is a little under 200 kilometres.

Croatia and Bosnia and Herzegovina have a land border, and it is an external border of the European Union. That means a passport check on the way in and again on the way back. Carry the identity document that your nationality requires and keep it within reach of your seat. Waiting times depend on the day. Summer weekends and the mornings after public holidays are slower. Your driver knows the usual pattern and plans the day around it.

### What to see in Mostar

Give the town three to four hours. That is enough for the bridge, the old bazaar and a long lunch.

- **Stari Most.** Walk across the bridge and look at it from both banks. In summer, local divers jump from the top in front of a crowd.
- **Kujundžiluk.** The old bazaar street sells copperware, ceramics and sweets.
- **A mosque and a minaret.** A few of the old mosques welcome visitors and some have a minaret you can climb for a view over the river and the bridge.
- **The river terraces.** Many restaurants sit directly above the Neretva. Lunch here is part of the visit.

Bring a card and some cash. Bosnia and Herzegovina uses its own currency, and cards are accepted in most places in the centre, though small shops often prefer cash.

### Why a private transfer works well

Group excursions to Mostar often leave early, stop at several places and return late. A private car lets you choose the day yourself.

- **Your own timing.** You decide when to leave Split and how long to stay. If you want the bridge in the quiet morning light, you can be there early.
- **Door to door.** You are collected at your hotel or at Split Airport and brought back to the same place.
- **Room for everyone.** A sedan takes up to four passengers and a Mercedes V-Class up to seven. For larger groups a Renault Trafic van seats up to eight. Everything stays in the car while you walk through town.
- **A calm border.** The driver handles the road and the queue. You only need your documents ready.

If you land in the morning and want to go straight to Mostar, tell us your flight number. We can plan the day from the airport.

### Planning the day

Leave Split early, ideally by eight in the morning, so that you reach Mostar before the busiest hours. Spend the middle of the day in the old town, eat lunch above the river, and start back by late afternoon. In July and August, plan for heat: wear light clothes and comfortable shoes, because the old paving is uneven and slippery.

Mostar also fits as a stop on a longer journey. Guests continuing to Dubrovnik can pass through Mostar and spend a few hours there on the way. Our Split to Dubrovnik guide explains the road along the coast, and the same driver can plan either version.

### A sample day

This is only a guide, and we shape it around you. Times are approximate and depend on the border queue.

- **About 08:00.** Collection at your hotel in Split or at Split Airport.
- **About 10:30 to 11:00.** Arrival in Mostar. Start with the bridge and the old bazaar while it is still quiet.
- **Midday.** Lunch above the river, then time for the mosques and a slow walk through the lanes.
- **About 15:00.** Start the drive back, with the border check on the way.
- **About 18:00.** Back at your hotel, or at the airport for an evening flight.

If you travel with children or with older guests, build in more time at lunch. The old town is compact, but the stone streets are uneven and the afternoon heat in summer is tiring.

The border is reached from the Makarska side as well, so a day from there is possible and the same planning applies. ## FAQ (5)

**1. How long does it take to drive from Split to Mostar?**
Allow roughly two and a half hours each way, depending on traffic and on the wait at the border. Most guests plan about nine to ten hours for the whole day, including three to four hours in Mostar.

**2. Do I need a passport to go to Mostar from Split?**
Yes, you cross an external border of the European Union into Bosnia and Herzegovina, so carry the identity document your nationality requires. Check the entry rules for your passport before you travel.

**3. Can I visit Mostar on the way from Split to Dubrovnik?**
Yes. Many guests combine the two. You can leave Split, spend a few hours in Mostar and continue to Dubrovnik the same day, or do it in the other direction. Contact us with your plan and we arrange it.

**4. Is a private driver better than a group tour to Mostar?**
It depends on what you want. A group tour has a fixed timetable and several stops. A private car lets you choose your departure time, how long you stay and where you eat. You also travel only with your own party.

**5. What is the best time of year to visit Mostar?**
Spring and autumn are the most comfortable, with milder temperatures. Summer is busy and hot, so go early in the day. In winter the town is quiet and cold, and some small shops keep shorter hours.
