Best Time to Visit Split: A Season-by-Season Guide

Split's character shifts sharply with the seasons — the same old town that feels sleepy in March can be packed to the walls of Diocletian's Palace by late July. Choosing when to visit matters as much as choosing where to stay.

Shoulder season: April–May and September–October

These months are generally considered the sweet spot: warm enough to swim by May and through most of September, with noticeably thinner crowds in the old town and lower accommodation prices than peak summer. Ferry schedules to the islands are usually running but less frequent than in July and August.

Peak summer: June–August

This is Split at its busiest — the harbour promenade, the palace basements and the beaches all draw large crowds, and both accommodation and car hire prices rise accordingly. It's also when the city feels most alive, with outdoor events and the longest daylight hours for day trips to the islands or south along the coast.

Winter: November–March

Split doesn't shut down in winter, but many seasonal restaurants and island ferry routes reduce or pause service. It suits travellers more interested in the old town's history and a quieter pace than in beach time, and flight options into Split Airport are noticeably reduced compared to summer.

Airport transfer availability

Private transfers run year-round regardless of season, though it's worth booking further ahead in July and August when demand for vehicles — particularly larger ones for groups — is highest.
