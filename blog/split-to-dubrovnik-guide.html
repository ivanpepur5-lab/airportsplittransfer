Split to Dubrovnik: The Complete Transfer Guide

The drive from Split to Dubrovnik covers roughly 230 kilometres of the Adriatic Highway, hugging the coastline for most of the route with the sea rarely out of view. Done as a private transfer, it takes around three and a half hours including the short border crossing through Neum.

The Neum corridor

Around halfway through the journey, the road passes through a narrow strip of Bosnia and Herzegovina's coastline at Neum — the country's only sea access. This means two short border checks, entering and re-entering Croatia, each usually taking only a few minutes outside peak summer weekends. It's worth carrying a passport even for what is otherwise a domestic-feeling drive.

Worth a stop along the way

The route passes close to Makarska and the Pelješac Peninsula, both easy detours if your schedule allows a longer day. Pelješac in particular is known for its vineyards and oyster farms around Ston, and a private transfer can build in a stop that a fixed-schedule bus simply can't.

Bus, flight or private transfer?

A direct flight between Split and Dubrovnik doesn't exist on most routes without a connection, and the intercity bus takes four to five hours with limited luggage space and no flexibility on timing. For travellers who value the scenery and want to arrive at a specific time, a private transfer covers the same distance with stops on request and a driver who knows the road.

Booking tips

Because this is a longer transfer, book at least a few days ahead where possible, and confirm luggage count clearly — the Mercedes V-Class comfortably handles a group of up to seven with full luggage, while larger parties are better served by the Renault Trafic.
